01 The problem

Texas law gives school boards an exclusive duty to govern student outcomes. Three failure modes get in the way.

Under TEC §11.151(b), trustees hold the exclusive power to govern. Under §11.1511 and §11.1515, they must adopt measurable goals, establish performance targets, and monitor progress. In practice, the artifacts that carry governance — agenda packets, strategic plans, monitoring reports — are produced by humans under pressure, with incomplete context, and the duty gets diluted.

MODE 01

Unmeasurable goals

Boards adopt strategic objectives without baselines, targets, dates, guardrails, or named owners. "Every student will grow" is aspiration, not governance. The statute requires measures.

MODE 02

Role drift

Boards are asked to approve execution details — "strategic actions," operational plans, staffing decisions — that belong to administration. Administration, in turn, is asked to set governance cadence. Everybody is doing someone else's job.

MODE 03

Broken feedback loop

A strategic plan is adopted in August. By October the monthly agenda has no visible tie to it. There is no scoreboard, no cadence, no early-warning signal when a guardrail is about to fall. The loop dies quietly.

When governance breaks, student outcomes follow. TEA intervention is the late-stage symptom of a problem that was already visible in the board book six months earlier.
02 The solution

A two-stage agent. Claude reads the board book
and writes the analysis. Gemini makes it conversational.

Stage one: the Claude pipeline ingests a board packet, maps every item to statute and to a codified body of governance principles, and produces a trustee-ready pre-read document in the same format an experienced trustee would produce by hand. Stage two: the board member opens that document and launches a live voice session — grounded in the same material, constrained by the same principles — that they can interrogate conversationally on the way to the meeting.

03 Stage one · the Claude pipeline

The analysis layer is a single Claude Agent SDK loop running four domain Skills.

The Claude Agent SDK gives the agent a computer: bash, file I/O, code execution, web search, and the ability to invoke custom Skills. A lead agent parses the board book, walks each agenda item through a deterministic loop — gather context, analyze against statute and principles, verify citations, assemble the pre-read — and writes the output as a structured markdown report.

The non-obvious design choice is Skills instead of a monolithic system prompt. Each Skill is a versioned markdown file that lives in the repository. Claude auto-loads the relevant Skill when a task matches its description. That means the governance IP is auditable, forkable, and improvable without redeploying the agent.

A trustee in another district who wants to adjust the risk-flagging heuristics for their local board norms edits the Skill file. A state-level reviewer who wants to add citation patterns for a new TEA framework adds them to the statute-mapper Skill. The agent inherits the improvement automatically.

This is how the governance agent stays a general tool and not a Brock-only curiosity.

04 The Skills stack

Four markdown files carry the entire domain intelligence.

Every piece of governance judgment — the statute citations, the risk patterns, the question-generation style, the output voice — lives in one of these four Skills. They are the open-source contribution. Districts fork the repo, tune the Skills to their local norms, and ship.

skills/statute-mapper/SKILL.md

Statute mapper

Maps every agenda item, proposed action, and piece of board language to the authorities that govern it: Texas Education Code, Texas Government Code (including TOMA), TAC, TEA frameworks, MSRB rules, and local Board Operating Procedures. Enforces citation verification — no authority appears in output without matching the bundled corpus.

Item K's blanket TOMA citation (551.071 – 551.087) is procedurally valid for agenda posting but the presiding officer must state the specific section before entering closed session (TGC §551.101).

skills/governance-principles/SKILL.md

Governance principles

The doctrine layer. Encodes the operator-level heuristics that separate good governance from theater. Every proposed objective is checked for a baseline, target, date, cadence, guardrail, and named owner. Every item is scored for role fit — is the board being asked to govern, or to execute?

The Strategic Plan item presents objectives and key strategic actions for adoption but defers measurable outcomes to a later reporting cycle. Flag: goals without measures violate §11.1511(b)(2)–(3).

skills/risk-flagger/SKILL.md

Risk flagger

The three-tier annotation system — RED FLAG, WATCH, POSITIVE — applied consistently across agenda items. Pattern-matches on known failure modes: blanket statutory citations, delegation without oversight, tariff or supply-chain exposure, quote-expiry pressure, pacing misses on scorecards, and more.

Tariff surcharge language in the bus procurement quote exposes the district beyond the TERP grant ceiling. WATCH: confirm vendor cap before 5/1 quote expiration.

skills/output-formatter/SKILL.md

Output formatter

Assembles the final artifact in one of two modes. The full pre-read mode produces a complete board-book analysis — executive summary table, per-item deep dive, numbered governance questions, meeting-prep checklist. The line-of-questioning mode produces a focused interrogation doc for a single issue, with strategic sequencing notes.

Full pre-read for a regular meeting · or a focused SAMCO-style line-of-questioning for a single contested item.

05 The doctrine

These aren't abstractions. They're the operator doctrine of a sitting trustee.

The governance-principles Skill carries real governance judgment — not a textbook model. Its v1 was extracted from thirty-plus email threads between a Brock ISD trustee and his board, a 166-page board book analysis produced by hand, two years of public-record artifacts, and one trip to the Weatherford Democrat about an agenda item so vague the public couldn't tell what was being decided. Six principles below. Ten more live in the skills file the agent loads at runtime.

“
Each principle below carries a rule (what the agent enforces), a canonical quote in the trustee's own voice (preserved in agent output), and a citation back to the Texas statute or local authority that grounds it.
Principle · 01

Policy controls over procedure

Board-adopted policy is the governing text. Operating procedures, administrative regulations, and third-party guidance cannot narrow or revise policy text adopted in open session.

TASB is not a legal authority. It is a member organization that provides model language and guidance. The controlling authority here is Texas law and Brock ISD's Board-adopted policy.
TEC §11.151(b) · BE(LOCAL) · January 2026 agenda dispute
Principle · 02

Transparent deliberation

Substantive board discussion happens in public session. Pre-clearing questions with the President or Superintendent circumvents public deliberation and is incompatible with TOMA.

Team of 8 does not mean trustees should avoid asking questions during meetings. It means we govern as a unified body once a vote is taken — not that we silence accountability before a vote happens.
TGC Ch. 551 (TOMA) · February 2026 board question protocol
Principle · 03

Measurable goals or no goal

A goal without a baseline, target, date, cadence, guardrail, and named owner is aspiration, not governance. The statute requires measures.

The objective says where we're going. The measure says how we'll know we got there. The Board adopts the objective, key results, guardrails, and review cadence — administration selects and adjusts actions to achieve the numbers.
TEC §11.1511(b)(2)–(3) · Strategic plan memorandum
Principle · 04

Specificity on the record

Agenda items must describe what the Board is being asked to do. Vague titles, blanket statutory citations, and items that obscure material change are governance failures in themselves.

The item listed on the agenda was so vague that the public had no idea what we were going to do, and that sits really unwell with me.
TGC §551.043 · February 2024 public comment policy fight
Principle · 05

Fiduciary urgency

Delay is not neutral. Students are making course decisions and life decisions in real time. When the Board can act and chooses to defer, it is accepting the cost of delay on behalf of the students it serves.

This Board has one job: govern in the best interest of students. Delaying even a discussion of AI and graduate readiness — when we can include it and our adopted policy supports trustee agenda access — undercuts the mission we were elected to carry out.
TEC §11.1515 · January 2026 AI-pathway discussion request
Principle · 06

Board governs, admin executes

The Board sets objectives, measures, guardrails, cadence, and standards. Administration selects and adjusts actions to achieve the numbers and reports results. Role drift reduces accountability in both directions.

We do not need to approve every strategic action — that's day-to-day execution. Our job is to set the standard, the target, and the review schedule. Administration brings forward the actions they'll use to hit the targets and reports results openly.
TEC §§11.1512, 11.201(d) · Strategic plan memorandum
06 Sample output · Brock ISD

The acceptance test is a direct comparison against a hand-written trustee pre-read.

For the Brock ISD April 13, 2026 regular meeting, the trustee produced a 166-page board book analysis by hand. The agent's output is measured against that document — same structure, same citation discipline, same risk-flagging voice. A representative excerpt:

Item K · Closed Session (TGC §§551.071 – 551.087)
Blanket TOMA citation on agenda
Type · Informational · TOMA compliance review

WATCH The blanket citation gives the board maximum flexibility but also means almost any topic could potentially be discussed in closed session. Under TGC §551.101 the presiding officer must announce the specific section(s) on the record before entering closed session — a blanket citation is acceptable for posting but not for the convening announcement.

RED FLAG TGC §551.074 does not authorize discussing general employment policy, salary schedules, or working conditions. It covers specific individuals by name or position only. If discussion veers into general policy, it belongs in open session.

Will the board president state the specific TGC sections — not just the blanket range — before entering closed session, as TGC §551.101 requires?
Have affected employees been notified of their right under TGC §551.074(b) to request a public hearing?

The agent produces output that is indistinguishable in format and discipline from the hand-written version — same executive summary table, same per-item structure, same RED FLAG / WATCH / POSITIVE taxonomy, same citation-verification footer. What's different is that it runs in under a minute on any board book in Texas instead of four hours per meeting, per trustee.

07 Stage two · the Gemini Live layer

A voice companion with a 128k window pre-loaded with exactly one board book.

Once the pre-read document is produced, the board member can launch a live voice session directly from it. Gemini 3.1 Flash Live opens a bidirectional audio WebSocket with a context window pre-populated with the board book, the generated pre-read, and a trimmed excerpt of the governance principles. A narrowly scoped system prompt turns the model into an explanatory agent, constrained to that one document.

Why this works

The context is the guardrail.

Most voice AI failure modes come from the model drawing on training-data priors when it should be drawing on the user's material. By pre-loading exactly one board book into a 128k window and instructing the model to stay within it, the behavior collapses to the narrow task: explain this document, apply these principles.

Why native audio

No transcription stack.

Gemini Live uses native audio-to-audio — no STT, no TTS, no intermediate text. That collapses the voice latency that historically made AI voice assistants feel like they were on a conference call. Response feels conversational, not turn-based.

Why the handoff

Claude assembles, Gemini narrates.

The heavy analytical lift — multi-step reasoning, cross-referencing statute, verifying citations — happens in Claude over roughly a minute of processing. The output document then becomes the shared reference both the trustee and Gemini can work from for the rest of the day.

Real-world use

The commute to the meeting.

The trustee drives to the board meeting. They tap "ask" on tonight's pre-read and spend the drive in natural conversation — "walk me through item seven," "what should I ask the superintendent about the scorecard," "read me the closing statement." They arrive briefed.

08 The two-model hybrid · design rationale

Each model plays to its strength.

This is not about religious preference for one vendor. It's about matching the task to the model. Claude is the best tool for multi-step analytical reasoning, careful citation work, and document assembly. Gemini Live is the best tool for real-time, low-latency, voice-native conversation. Putting both on the same artifact gives the end user the best of each.

Claude · Stage 01

Analysis

Runs offline in roughly a minute. Produces a polished document that a trustee or conservator would be willing to sign their name to. This is Claude's sweet spot: long-horizon reasoning, tool use, structured output.

  • Multi-step execution loop via Agent SDK
  • Four auto-loaded Skills with domain knowledge
  • Bundled corpus of Texas statute
  • Citation verification discipline
  • Produces the canonical artifact
Gemini · Stage 02

Conversation

Runs live. The context window is narrow — one board book, one pre-read, trimmed principles. Native audio means the conversation feels real. The document is the guardrail.

  • Bidirectional WebSocket, native audio-to-audio
  • System instruction scopes to the document
  • 128k token context pre-loaded at session start
  • Voice activity detection, natural interruption
  • Default session 10 minutes — a full commute

12 Why this matters

Governance is the unoptimized layer of public education. Outcomes follow governance quality.

Texas has roughly twelve hundred public school districts. Each one is governed by an elected board of trustees who, by statute, hold the exclusive duty to govern student outcomes. Most of those boards are run by volunteers with day jobs, working from board packets of a hundred and sixty pages, meeting once a month. They do not lack commitment. They lack leverage.

An AI agent that turns a hundred-and-sixty-page board book into a structured trustee pre-read, in under a minute, grounded in statute and in hard-won governance principles, is that leverage. Open-sourced, it's leverage for every trustee in the state. Delivered with a voice companion that travels with the board member to the meeting, it's leverage where and when they need it.

If a governance agent shipping in a Texas district in April raises the floor of board work by even ten percent, the downstream effect on student outcomes is measurable within a single academic year.
