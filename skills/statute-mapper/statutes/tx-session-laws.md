# Texas Session Laws — selected enabling statutes

Session laws (House Bills, Senate Bills) create or modify code sections.
Once enacted, the operative text lives in the amended code — but the
history matters for governance analysis, because what a legislature
created it can also modify or repeal.

Used by RF_21 (legislative funding dependency) when the district has
committed to multi-year obligations backed by a revenue stream that a
future session could alter.

**Common usage in board context:** Cited as the origin of Hold Harmless, TIA, and the 2019 school-finance restructuring. When a bond or budget item relies on a Hold Harmless revenue stream, RF_21 flags that the stream is a statutory construct — not a constitutional guarantee — and the 2019 session created it.

**Cross-references:** HB3 (2019); TEC Ch. 42.

**Common usage in board context:** Cited when a district committed HB2-derived salary increases to permanent teacher compensation. RF_21 flags this as structural exposure: if the 2027 (90th) legislature modifies or sunsets HB2, the district inherits an unfunded salary obligation. The April 13, 2026 Brock packet's Item 5 documents $1.18M in restricted HB2 salary already committed.
