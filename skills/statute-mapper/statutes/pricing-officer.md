# Pricing Officer Delegation (TGC §§1207.007, 1371.053)

The statutory authority for delegating bond-sale pricing decisions to a
named Pricing Officer (typically the Superintendent or CFO). These sections
**authorize** delegation; they do not **require** the Board to exclude
itself from oversight. Pairs with Principle 16 (role clarity) and Risk
Pattern #2 (delegation of execution authority without Board oversight
mechanism).

---

### TGC §1207.007

**Title.** Delegation of Authority to Pricing Officer — refunding bonds.

<!-- TODO: verbatim text of TGC §1207.007 goes here as a blockquote. -->
<!-- Source: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.1207.htm#1207.007 -->

**Cross-references:** TGC §1371.053 (Pricing Officer delegation generally); TEC §45.001 (bond issuance authority of the board).

**Common usage in board context:** Cited when a refunding order delegates final pricing to a Pricing Officer. The delegation is lawful; the question the agent flags is whether the Board retains meaningful oversight — parameters, reporting cadence, post-sale review.

### TGC §1371.053

**Title.** Authority of Issuer to Delegate — general Pricing Officer authority for public securities.

<!-- TODO: verbatim text of TGC §1371.053 goes here as a blockquote. -->
<!-- Source: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.1371.htm#1371.053 -->

**Cross-references:** TGC §1207.007 (refunding-specific delegation); MSRB G-42 (advisor fiduciary duty to the governing body).

**Common usage in board context:** Cited on new-money issuances that delegate final pricing parameters to a Pricing Officer. Same question as §1207.007: what oversight does the Board preserve? If the delegation bounds (max principal, max TIC, expiration date) are missing or loose, the Board has handed the pricing decision to one person with no checkpoint. That's Risk Pattern #2.
