# TEA Frameworks

The Texas Education Agency publishes several governance and
effectiveness frameworks. None of these are statute. They are TEA
guidance or adopted practice patterns — useful context, not controlling
authority. When an argument needs controlling authority, cite TEC.

Pairs with Principle 1 (policy controls over procedure) — TASB and TEA
framework guidance cannot override board-adopted policy.

---

### LSG (Lone Star Governance)

**What it is.** TEA's governance framework for Texas school boards, centered on a target that the Board spend approximately 50% of its meeting time on student outcomes (the "student outcomes" orientation). Developed in partnership with the Council of Great City Schools; curriculum delivered through TEA-approved training cohorts.

**Common usage in board context.** Cited when a board is being asked whether its agenda allocation matches the 50%-target. Also cited when training is invoked to override adopted policy — see Principle 1.

**Not a legal authority.** LSG is a framework, not statute. A board that participates in LSG training still governs under TEC, TGC, and its own adopted policy. LSG materials may be referenced as normative guidance; they do not displace controlling law.

**Caveat.** "Team of 8" is an LSG-adjacent training concept. It does **not** mean trustees should avoid asking questions in meetings, pre-clear questions, or suppress dissent — see principles.md §I Principle 4 for the correct interpretation.

---

### ESF (Effective Schools Framework)

**What it is.** TEA's district- and campus-level effectiveness framework. Identifies "essential actions" in five levers (strong school leadership, effective curriculum, data-driven instruction, etc.). Tied to state and federal improvement requirements for districts and campuses with low ratings.

**Common usage in board context.** Cited when an improvement plan or strategic plan draws on ESF essential-action language. Also cited in TEA monitoring contexts.

**Not a legal authority.** The Board's statutory improvement-planning home is TEC §11.251, not ESF. ESF is a menu of practices, not a mandate.

---

### FIRST (Financial Integrity Rating System of Texas)

**What it is.** The state's annual financial-health rating for school districts. Produces indicator-based scores (A–F scale, or "Superior/Above Standard/Standard/Substandard" in older vintages) based on audit, debt, and operating metrics.

**Common usage in board context.** Cited in annual financial reporting and in any discussion of district fiscal health. Often paired with TEC §44.002–44.006 context.

**Not a legal authority.** FIRST is a rating. Board fiscal-management duties flow from TEC Ch. 44, not from FIRST indicators. A strong FIRST rating does not immunize a weak budget amendment; a weak FIRST rating is a signal, not a verdict.

---

### Team of 8

**What it is.** A TEA governance-training concept referring to the seven trustees plus the superintendent acting as a coordinated team on behalf of students. Taught in LSG and related cohorts.

**Correct interpretation.** "Team of 8" means the Board governs as a unified body **once a vote is taken**. It does **not** mean:

- Trustees should avoid asking questions during meetings
- Trustees should pre-clear questions with the President or Superintendent
- The Board should project artificial unanimity by suppressing legitimate oversight

The Texas Open Meetings Act (TGC Ch. 551) requires substantive deliberation in public session. Pre-clearing questions or suppressing dissent before a vote is incompatible with TOMA — see principles.md §I Principle 4.

**Common usage in board context.** Cited (and often misapplied) to discourage public questioning. When the agent sees "Team of 8" invoked to suppress accountability, flag as RED FLAG (Risk Pattern #4 territory, depending on context).

**Not a legal authority.** Like all TEA training concepts, Team of 8 is guidance. The controlling law is TGC Ch. 551 (TOMA) and board-adopted policy.

---

### TAPR (Texas Academic Performance Report)

**What it is.** TEA's annual district- and campus-level performance report. Contains academic outcomes, demographic information, college/career/military readiness indicators, and staff data. Published each fall for the prior school year.

**Common usage in board context.** Cited when the Board is reviewing annual performance data, particularly in the statutorily-required annual public performance presentation (TEC §39.306). Pairs with Principle 10 (transparent data, full population) — TAPR reports on the full student population by subgroup.

**Not a legal authority.** TAPR is a data product. The Board's duty to publicly present district performance each year is grounded in TEC §39.306.
